## Substack Title

## Executive Summary

## Cross-Asset Themes
- Hormuz runs through four books: the four traffic dates, the gas contracts, the December Fed contracts and the Iran deal contracts. An escalation helps the traffic positions and hurts the gas positions; a sudden settlement does the reverse. The two sets partly offset and should be sized together.
- The four Hormuz dates are one view at four strikes, all HIGH. If traffic recovers early, all four lose together; size them as a single position.
- The December half-point position and the gas positions share an oil driver: a crude spike would lift both the inflation case for a larger hike and the gas contracts. A soft November jobs report in early December would undo the half-point case.
- The Citrini position runs against the Fed: a hiking cycle into an oil shock with a ten-year yield above 5% is the path most likely to produce the deep recession the scenario needs.
- Lutnick, Hassett and Hegseth are one question about post-midterm turnover. All three rest on AFG's 24-case denial study, which is small, and a December wave of exits would move them together.
- The Heisman positions form one book that wins if the sportsbooks are right and loses together if Kalshi is; the Smith and Carr estimates moved sharply this week because the books did.
- The Bond positions form one book. If Pattinson is cast, the Pattinson leg loses and the Turner and Lowden legs win. If Turner or Lowden is cast, that leg loses and the other two win. If anyone else is cast, all three win.
- Grand Theft Auto VI's reviews on November 18th land after the Game Awards nominations on November 17th; a weak reception would help the Game of the Year position, a historic one would hurt it.
